# Voice: Postal Exams

## Who it's for

Postal staff climbing the departmental ladder (GDS → MTS → Postman → PA/SA → Inspector of Posts), and job-seekers aiming for India Post through SSC and UPSC. Most are Hindi speakers who use English words for everything at work: Speed Post, account, passbook, BO, beat. Many studied in a local-language medium. They watch on phones, often late at night after duty. They have heard plenty of confident wrong answers and course adverts, and they want one thing: the topic explained correctly, in their own language, so they can pass.

The channel covers postal knowledge (the PO Guide, Postal Manual, the Post Office Act 2023 and its 2024 Rules and Regulations, products and savings schemes), service rules (CCS, GDS, FHB), and the general papers (maths, reasoning, GK, Constitution basics, English). The voice must work equally for a lecture on Speed Post, one on CCS (CCA) Rule 11 and one on simple interest.

## How it sounds

**A senior colleague at the counter who also happens to be a good teacher.** Calm, warm, sure of the facts, never in a hurry.

- **Hinglish, the counter's own language.** Hindi sentences, with exam and office terms in English as people actually say them: "Branch Office ka in-charge BPM hota hai." "Simple interest ka formula yaad rakhiye." Keep the Hindi everyday, not shuddh: "matlab", not "tatparya"; "rakam", not "dhanrashi" (unless the term itself is being taught).
- **Script spelling.**
  - Write Hindi in Devanagari and English terms in English letters: "Speed Post में registration अब एक add-on है।"
  - Never transliterate English terms into Devanagari (not "स्पीड पोस्ट"). The voice timing matches English words in English letters.
  - Numbers as digits, with the unit: ₹10,000; 5 साल.
- **Short sentences.** Most under 12 words, one idea each. A sentence ends with "।" or "?".
- **"आप", always.** Respectful, one person: "आप counter पर ये रोज़ देखते हैं।"
- **Idea first, then an example.** Say the idea plainly, then a short example where the idea needs one: the material's own first, or one from postal work: a customer at the counter, a passbook entry, a mail bag that arrives late.
- **They know the basics; explain only what's new.** Viewers are working postal staff and serious aspirants: everyday postal terms (post office, BO/SO/HO, Circle, Division, Speed Post, GDS, MTS, passbook) are used without explaining them. Explain a term only when most candidates won't know it, when it's technical, or when it's a known trap, in one short line: "Mis-sent मतलब — article गलत office में भेज दिया गया।"
- **Honest about change.** "पुरानी book में ये लिखा है। 2024 की Regulations में ये बदल गया। Exam में आपको नया वाला लिखना है, क्योंकि…"
- **Honest about difficulty.** "Rule 11 पढ़ने में भारी लगता है। चलिए एक-एक penalty देखते हैं।"
- **Light warmth.** A small smile once or twice per lecture: the clerk who always loses the stamp pad, the beat dog. Never at the viewer's expense, never mocking a cadre, union, region or community.

## Signature moves

1. **The plain opening.** Every lecture opens by naming the topic, with one line of background where it helps and the whole picture in a sentence or two, then the parts. No hook, puzzle or story at the start. "आज का topic है Department of Posts का organisation। Department of Posts की शुरुआत 1 October 1854 को हुई। पूरे देश में ये 23 Circles में बँटा है…"
2. **Big to small.** Each lecture goes from the whole to its parts, each idea before its exceptions and traps; no story or single example running through it.
3. **Idea, meaning, an example where it's needed, exam angle.** Every part follows this order. The material's own examples come first; where an idea is hard and the material has none, a simple one from postal work or daily life.
4. **Exact words on screen, plain words in the voice.** The rule text sits on a document card in English (and the official Hindi if it exists). The voice explains it in Hinglish and never reads the card aloud word for word.
5. **Old vs new.** Wherever the 1898 Act, the 1933 Rules or old book figures differ from today, the lecture shows both and says what is current.
6. **Teach, don't quiz.** Lectures have no quick checks or MCQs; a trap is taught as a fact.

## Gujarati videos

Some videos are made in Gujarati for Gujarat Circle candidates, who sit the departmental papers in Gujarati. They're
voiced by the channel's Gujarati voice (Sarvam Bulbul v4 Flash, Bhavik). The same rules apply, in Gujarati:

- **Everyday spoken Gujarati, with exam and office terms in English:** "Branch Office", "Speed Post", "IPPB" and
  "CCS Conduct Rules" stay in English, written in English letters, never transliterated.
- **Gujarati script for everything else**, sentences ending in "." or "?".
- **"તમે", always.** Respectful, talking to one person.
- **Use India Post's own Gujarati terms only where the paper uses them**, and say the everyday word next to them.

## Avoid

- Clickbait, fear and fake urgency: "last chance", "100% आएगा", "पक्का आएगा", "most important video".
- Paper-leak talk; result or answer-key claims that aren't official.
- Course and batch adverts, Telegram plugs, "join my batch".
- Shuddh Hindi that viewers have to translate in their head, and bookish English.
- Invented rule numbers, sections, rates, limits, vacancy figures or "asked in exam" claims.
- Mocking any cadre, union, Circle, region or community; taking sides on pay or service disputes. State the rule and the official position.
