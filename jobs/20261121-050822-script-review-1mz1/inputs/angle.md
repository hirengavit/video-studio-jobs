# Prohibited and restricted articles: the plan

## The plan

**Promise.** By the end, the viewer can sort any article at the counter into three boxes: never by post (prohibited), only on conditions (restricted), and suspected (stop it, report it, and follow the lawful procedure). They will know each condition, number and authority the exam asks.

**Opening (R1).** Name the topic plainly: prohibited and restricted articles. One line of background: the Post Office Act 2023 and the Post Office Rules and Regulations 2024, in force from 16 December 2024. The whole picture in two sentences: some articles can never go by post; some go only when a condition is met; and a suspected article has a lawful way to be stopped, opened and disposed of. Then the ten parts, read as groups from a map card.

**Chapters, in the material's own order (big to small, idea before trap):**

1. **Topic and the whole picture.** Topic, Act and Rules with the date, the two-line picture, the map.
2. **Part 1 · Prohibited vs restricted, and the law.** Why the post refuses some articles (staff, public, other mail, equipment); the four grounds for refusal; the two definitions with their Hindi terms; explosive-in-a-box vs jewellery as the example; Act 2023, Rules 2024, Regulations 2024 with amendments, instructions only where consistent; Regulation 25 = domestic prohibitions, the Regulations also cover international exceptions and unlawful, intercepted, dangerous and opened articles; Rule 16 = interception, detention, opening; other laws still apply; the five conditions to read with every exception (nature, protection, special process, transport, scope). Exam angle: strong packing and insurance never remove a legal prohibition.
3. **Part 2 · Dangerous goods, air restriction and flammables.** Definition of dangerous goods and its five named kinds; the wider prohibition (filthy, noxious, deleterious, injuring mail or staff, soiling mail and equipment); danger from contents or packing (sulphuric acid; a leaking harmless liquid); RDX and firecrackers; turpentine and varnish; infectious material; medicines and cosmetics; a pistol to a friend. Batteries: surface mode only, and surface-only does not mean admissible; IATA, ICAO, wildlife and the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940. Flash point below 200°F with the spirits, turpentine and varnish; matches in the explanation of explosives with the two Acts; flash point meaning; "below", not "200 or below"; the oxalic acid question and why it is not permission. Trap: ordinary medicines are not narcotics.
4. **Recap · Parts 1 and 2.** Envelope card, five keywords.
5. **Part 3 · Sharp instruments, smells, liquids, rakhi and pins.** "Not properly protected" with the needle example; asafetida in a hermetically sealed tin or metal case (what fails); liquids are not one class; short recap of the 2-receptacle packing; India Post Parcel not accepted if specifications are not met, and its declaration (no form number in the rule); rakhi and special rakhi envelopes, precious metal brings insurance; pins: return at once at posting, later remove and return through RLO; an unsafe article in transit is not passed on. Trap: "all liquids are prohibited" is too broad.
6. **Part 4 · Prohibited messages, lottery and the address side.** Obscene material inside or on the cover (forms on a table); words and designs on the cover (seditious, scurrilous explained); law and order; pictures of a proclaimed offender or a convicted person, with "any applicable law"; lottery matter and the Central or State Government exception, with the outside declaration; three kinds of address-side matter; one delivery address (short recap); counterfeit, pirated, goods barred in origin or destination, another person's current correspondence (archives excepted), UPU and Central Government prohibitions; printed papers and literature for the blind; Press and Registration of Periodicals Act 2023 and the Press Registrar General of India; an old invalid stamp and stamp misuse.
7. **Recap · Parts 3 and 4.**
8. **Part 5 · Live creatures and medical consignments.** General rule and both wrong statements; international letter post other than insured items: bees, leeches, silkworms, insect-control organisms and Drosophilidae between recognised institutions; international parcels by the countries' rules; the reciprocal dangerous-goods exception; bee packing; medical material is not a live animal; the five conditional medical arrangements on a table, with the packing for viscera and rabid brains, plague cultures (senders, recipient, certificate), anthrax vaccine, tuberculosis (3/4 inch); osmic acid and perfume with their numbers and red labels. Trap: a scientific purpose alone does not remove the dangerous-goods prohibition.
9. **Part 6 · Narcotics and local prohibitions.** INCB narcotics and psychotropic substances, other illicit drugs; psychotropic explained; insurance is not a licence; what a local prohibition is and its scope; the local list (drugs between places in India; Port Blair and Nancowry bird skins with the ostrich and specimen exceptions, fans and boas; Punjab and Delhi); San Jose scale states on a table, Icerya purchasi, banana and bunchy top mosaic; Kutch and the Conservator of Forests, Kashmir; government and licensed-chemist drug consignments with their declarations. Trap: keep origin, destination and mode.
10. **Recap · Parts 5 and 6.**
11. **Part 7 · Coins, currency, jewellery and insurance.** Special process (RBI, FEMA currency regulations 2015 under FEMA 1999); currency notes, coins and banknotes only insured; what may not go uninsured in letter post; definitions of precious metals, stones, bullion, bearer security; actual value and the sender's declaration; the limits table; insurance not above real value and documents at replacement cost; the ₹3 lakh and ₹25,000 examples; the two dates (1 October 2025, 1 April 2026) said once; short recap of the definitions (watches, articles of gold or silver, cut counterfeit coins, defaced notes); a country may bar insured gold bullion. Trap: registration is not insurance.
12. **Part 8 · Suspected article: danger and offence.** Detain where detected and inform police, postal and other authorities at once; proof not needed; the four offence grounds; competent authority; disposal as the enforcement agency directs, no personal power; opening needs law enforcement present, not the addressee; India Post Parcel detained at the nearest post office. Trap: never forward a suspected dangerous article.
13. **Recap · Parts 7 and 8.**
14. **Part 9 · Interception: who orders, how long, what is recorded.** Section 9 and its six grounds; interception, detention, opening; the authority table; Circle Head and Divisional Head explained; issuing vs executing; reasons in writing; emergency: 3 and 7 working days, ceases without confirmation; 30 days from issue, 90 maximum, then handed over; the six records; the review committee and its 3-month meeting; foreign articles and other laws.
15. **Part 10 · Disposal, confidentiality, customs and liability.** Substantiated vs not; forwarded as if unopened; notice to sender, else addressee; confidentiality and its two exceptions; international admission, transit country law, removal of inadmissible contents; customs, duty at delivery from the addressee, ₹30 fees and the 500 g line; duty does not lift a prohibition; liability and compensation exclusions, force majeure; officer liability preserved; PO Guide Part II Sections IX and X, Customs Act Section 11, no refund on lawful confiscation; the whole decision in order.
16. **Recap · Parts 9 and 10.**
17. **याद रखने वाली बातें.** The material's key points first, as correct facts in simple sentences.
18. **Close.** One warm line about the counter.

**Ends** with the plain recap and one warm line; no mention of other lectures. Topics an earlier lecture taught in full (liquid packing, asafetida, pins, one delivery address, insurance limits and definitions) get a few lines each, said as plain facts.
