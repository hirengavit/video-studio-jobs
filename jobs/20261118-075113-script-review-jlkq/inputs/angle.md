# Department of Posts structure, circles and their headquarters

## The plan

**Promise.** By the end, the viewer can place every office of the Department of Posts on one ladder, from the Ministry and Dak Bhawan down to a Sub-division, name all 23 civil Circles with their headquarters and territories, keep the Base Circle and the specialised offices apart, and avoid the look-alike traps (Secretary vs DGPS, Secretary (Posts) vs Secretary (PSB), state capital vs Circle headquarters, the Board names).

**Opening (R1).** The topic named plainly. One line of background: the Department grew from the unified postal administration of 1 October 1854. The whole picture in two sentences: the Ministry of Communications on top, Dak Bhawan with the Postal Directorate and the Postal Services Board, then 23 civil Circles, each with Regions, Divisions and Sub-divisions, plus one Base Circle for the Army. Then the six parts, numbered. No hook, no story, no exam names.

**Chapters, in the material's own order (big to small):**

1. **Topic and map.** The opening above and the six parts.
2. **Part 1 · Department and central administration.** Ministry of Communications and what India Post provides; Minister, Minister of State, first Communications Minister (Rafi Ahmed Kidwai); Secretary (Posts) heads the Department and chairs the PSB; Dak Bhawan address; Postal Directorate and its work; "D" Section as Central Bag Office; DGPS and operations; duties revised 27 March 2026 (not every administrative matter is the DG's); appointment by the President vs the Central Government; the DG's powers under the Act (regulations) and Rule 4 (products, collaboration); exam angle on the wording "Head of the Department", "Chairperson", "Head of postal operations". The legal framework: Act 2023 with its dates, Rules and Regulations 2024 in force 16 December 2024, what they replaced (one line each, as events). History: Lord Dalhousie and stamps, RMS 1854, APS 1856, Postal Manual 1860, and the Department's vision.
3. **Part 2 · Postal Services Board.** Apex policy-making body (rule 5), also called the apex management body; perpetual succession and common seal in plain words; the Central Government may specify composition and duties by Gazette notification (rule 6), and the rule fixes no permanent number; ex officio Chairperson; 1 Chairperson and 7 Members (table on screen, voice names the count and the two that come back later); Member (CS); portfolio names change, with the 2024 rename of ADG (Coordination) to Member (Coordination); Financial Adviser as permanent invitee, head of IFW, AS&FA as one combined designation, the post listed as JS&FA; Secretary (PSB) vs Secretary (Posts); DDGs, Directors, ADGs, and DDG ≠ DGPS.
4. **Recap card 1.** Four keywords from Parts 1 and 2.
5. **Part 3 · Circle to Sub-division.** Circle, Region, Division, Sub-division and their heads (table); a simple chain example; the Rules' definitions of Circle Head, Regional Head, Regional Director, Divisional Head (wider than SPOs, but SPOs or SSPOs is the usual answer); the definitions of "Postmaster General", "Superintendent" and "Director (Postal Services)"; Postal vs RMS Divisions, RMS meaning and its four modes; SPOs/SSPOs vs SRM/SSRM; RMS Sub-division head; the 2026 common structure (Head of Region at PMG or Director level, Head of Logistics, who reports to whom, what comes under logistics, JAG or SAG level, implementation dates); control vs appointment, the 17 February 2025 powers, Head of Sub-division as engaging authority for GDS ABPM and Dak Sevak only; the CPMG fixing division jurisdiction with the Circle IFA, and the 23 Postal Accounts Offices as IFA; network numbers on a table.
6. **Part 4 · 23 Circles, headquarters and territories.** What headquarters means; the full table on screen; the pairs that matter (Gujarat, Haryana and Punjab, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, Assam and North East, Karnataka and Mysuru, Kerala); Jammu and Kashmir at Srinagar with a Circle Office at Jammu; older English names. Then jurisdiction and co-terminus; the exception table on screen, taught in the material's groups: Gujarat with Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu (merger 26 January 2020, Daman not Maharashtra), Maharashtra with Goa, J&K with Ladakh, North East's 6 states (Assam and Sikkim outside), West Bengal with Sikkim and Andaman and Nicobar, Puducherry split three ways, Kerala with Lakshadweep (Laccadive) and Mahe, Punjab with Chandigarh; postal arrangement ≠ constitutional status; the material's Daman employee example.
7. **Recap card 2.** Keywords from Parts 3 and 4.
8. **Part 5 · Base Circle and Army Postal Service.** APS as the Base Circle; 23 + 1; head ADG APS, Major General; APS Directorate at Sena Dak Bhawan, 1856; Defence Minister; deputation; 75% and 25% of other ranks only; APO and FPO; the 2 CBPOs with APO, city and PIN; PIN digit 9; what 56 APO and 99 APO serve; "2 CBPOs" is not the whole network.
9. **Part 6 · Special offices, training, Guide and other Boards.** Territorial unit vs specialised office; PLI Directorate (1884, Chanakyapuri) vs Director PLI Kolkata; Parcel Directorate (2018, Malcha Marg, CGM); Business Development Directorate (1996) as a former name; CCS & RB Directorate and Secretary (PSB) from 19 August 2026; Head of Logistics is not a national directorate; PSD, CSD, MMS, Foreign Post and their heads and counts; CEPT, its locations, Disaster Recovery Centre and Post Info; RAKNPA; 6 PTCs (table), Vadodara, Mysuru trap, 7th PTC at Shivpuri; PO Guide parts and Part I; the five Boards and who leads each.
10. **याद रखने वाली बातें.** Six short points: Secretary vs DGPS, PSB 1 + 7, 23 civil + 1 Base, the headquarters pairs, the territorial exceptions, the Board chairs; each said only as the correct fact.
11. **Close.** One warm line.

**Ending.** The recap, then one warm line about holding the whole ladder in mind. No mention of other lectures.
