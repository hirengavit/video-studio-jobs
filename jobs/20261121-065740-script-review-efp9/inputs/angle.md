# Letters, Letter Cards and Postcards: the plan

## The plan

**Promise.** By the end, the viewer can take a letter, a letter card or a postcard at the counter, say which product it is, check its weight, size and build against the current limits, and work out the right charge when a condition is broken or the postage is short.

**Opening (R1).** The topic named plainly: letters, letter cards and postcards, the three forms of sending a message by post. One line of background: their domestic rules sit in Chapter V of the Post Office Regulations 2024, in force from 16 December 2024. The whole picture in two lines: each form has its own conditions (weight, size, build, postage); when a condition is broken, the charge follows a set calculation. Then the map of ten parts on screen.

**Chapters, in the material's own order (big to small, idea before its traps):**

1. **Topic and big picture** — as above, with the numbered map.
2. **Part 1 · Three forms, product and service** — letter, letter card, postcard defined; the physical difference (envelope, own cover, open); "letter post" is the wider term (postcard and book post are their own products); the product tells which conditions to check, the service decides booking, tracking and proof of delivery; accountable and unaccountable items; APT 2.0 barcode induction and invoicing, Dak Sewa App delivery record, bulk mailers' barcode series and Excel booking data, nationwide rollout reported 7 July 2026; trap: a barcode alone does not make ordinary mail accountable; prepayment as the general rule, impressed stamp on stationery and extra stamps for the difference.
3. **Part 2 · Letter: weight and size** — 500 g maximum; weight and size are separate tests; roll vs other than roll and what is measured; the four limits (roll 100–800 mm and 170–1000 mm; non-roll 600 mm and 900 mm, minimum face 140 × 90 mm ±2 mm); the roll example (750 + 2 × 125 = 1000 passes; with 130 mm, 1010 fails); trap: 600 × 300 × 300 is not the letter formula.
4. **Part 3 · The envelope** — the 600 mm general limit does not replace the envelope size; envelope 140 × 90 to 458 × 324 mm ±2 mm; the eight specifications on a table (voice names the groups, and the GSM and thickness pair); GSM vs thickness as two different measures; the white "Safety films non-inflammable" label, which does not lift the ban on dangerous contents.
5. **Recap · Parts 1–3** (envelope card, four keywords).
6. **Part 4 · Window envelope** — only the address block with its barcode visible; enclosure must not shift; window 35–50 × 80–125 mm; materials (glassine 35 GSM, cellophane 40 GSM, 15-micron PVC or BOPP, with the full names); paper envelope with a window, a wholly transparent envelope is not sent; margins 15 left, 15 bottom, 40 top; logo top left, stamps or frank top right, 10 mm from the address block; 15 mm bottom for barcode; 5 mm quiet space, logo up to 25 × 50, QR at least 18 × 18, code area 60 × 10; trap: window size and barcode space are separate tests; panel parallel to the longest side, readable, clear of date stamping; breach: double letter postage with credit.
7. **Part 5 · Inland Letter Card** — definition and ILC; written on the sheet itself; fully prepaid, 5 g, 70 GSM; unfolded and folded sizes with flaps; three flaps and their limits, flaps may go if another closing method works; private manufacture, closed on 3 sides, "Letter Card" at top left of the address side; nothing enclosed or attached, the postcard's thin-picture permission does not apply; trap: the 5 g limit does not permit an enclosure; breach: treated as a letter, double the deficiency, the ₹2.50 → ₹5 example; domestic stationery is not international postage, an unpaid outward international item comes back without charge.
8. **Recap · Parts 4–5.**
9. **Part 6 · Postcard conditions** — open communication, impressed stamp, no secrecy; five specifications (5 g, 140 × 90 mm = 14 × 9 cm, fully prepaid, "Post Card" in English or Hindi, not thinner or more flexible); private cards need card substance, "neither thinner" is a minimum, not an exact match; space on the card (right-hand half for address, postage, postal directions; stamp at top right, dotted space; left half and back for message or one- or many-colour advertisement; sender's address top left of the back); the four permitted attachments and where labels go; no folding, cover, cutting or altering, except initials perforated in the impressed stamp; in an envelope or folded it is no longer a postcard; trap: no ±2 mm tolerance on the domestic postcard; the international postcard sizes with 2 mm tolerance, also for airmail postcards; "Air Mail", "Late Fee Paid" and "To await arrival" may be written, so it can go poste restante; breach: treated as a letter, double the deficiency, minimum ₹1, paise rounded up to a rupee.
10. **Part 7 · One Postcard category and the former names** — one category, 50 paise; printed matter and adverts still meet all conditions; single postcard; replies (no folding, no attached card, a reply card meets all conditions); printed (printing, cyclostyling, mechanical; cyclostyling explained), Meghdoot (advert space next to the address, 4 colours, 14 × 9 cm), competition (response to a media competition, not a students' product); merged from 20 December 2024; trap: printing or a competition does not create a separate tariff; Business Reply Service is a different arrangement; history: first postcard in Austria 1 October 1869, India's East India Postcard 1879.
11. **Recap · Parts 6–7.**
12. **Part 8 · First-class, second-class mail and air** — transmission by administrative instructions: rail for long distance, road within a city, air only for geography or a case-by-case decision; first-class (letters, postcards, ILCs); second-class list of seven on a table; 10 classes, 9 letter mail and 1 parcel mail, so letter mail, letters and first-class are not the same; second-class by air needs the marking and the surcharge; registered newspaper: second-class, first-class only in sorting, "registered" means its postal registration; greeting cards and invitations concession (surface with first-class, marked below "Book Packet"); airmail fee ₹2 first 50 g, ₹1 each further 50 g, the 120 g = ₹4 example; blue label top left; parcels prepay the full air fee; acknowledgement card weight counts; limited airlift; Book Now Pay Later as a separate authorised arrangement; QMS; trap: sorting priority, surface with first-class and free airlift are three different things.
13. **Part 9 · Counter checks and dues** — the five counter checks, including how each is posted (box vs counter, a large ordinary item handed in stays ordinary); shortpayment vs condition breach as different triggers; deficiency defined; the general recovery rule (short recap: double the prepaid rate, double the deficiency, minimum ₹1, airmail fees too, from addressee or sender); the ₹5/₹3 → ₹4 example; the three condition-breach calculations on a table with current rates (letter ₹5 per 20 g, letter card ₹2.50, postcard 50 paise) and the three worked examples (₹5 more, ₹5, ₹9); trap: double postage with credit vs double the deficiency; who marks postage due (Postmaster, supervisor, delivery officials, Postman or authorised delivery person; in a BO the BPM and the delivering GDS); dues collected before delivery; outward international items (including letter cards and postcards) returned without charge; undelivered unpaid postcards and letter cards destroyed at once in the disposal of undelivered items.
14. **Recap · Parts 8–9.**
15. **Part 10 · A few more rules** — PO Guide Part I: Section I General, Section II Letter and Parcel Post (clause ranges on screen); Inland Post defined; letters prepaid and securely closed, an unpaid letter posted open is closed by the Post Office; perishable biological substances by letter post under the prescribed packing; Recorded Delivery is not a current service.
16. **याद रखने वाली बातें** — the material's key points first: limits of the three products, the envelope paper pair, the window rule, the letter card and postcard limits, one Postcard category at 50 paise, first-class without free airlift, barcode ≠ accountable, the two breach calculations. Each trap said only as the correct fact.
17. **Close** — one warm line, no mention of other lectures.

**Building on earlier lectures (R14).** Envelope closing and size, window envelopes, the general recovery rule, Business Reply and QMS were taught earlier; here they get short, plain recaps with every fact of this material, nothing contradicting them, and no mention of those lectures.

**How it ends.** The plain recap of the key points, then one warm line about looking at a postcard's right-hand half at the counter.
